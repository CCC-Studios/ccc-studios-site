Why create with AI?

AI can generate a thousand images. It doesn't know which one deserves to exist.

What is worth imagining? Which story deserves to be told? Why this choice rather than another? Those are the questions our workshops teach young people to ask.

A young person who rejects five images because none matches their idea is showing creativity, judgment and critical thinking. The learning happens in the decisions, not in the generated image.

At the end, we want them to think: “I had an idea. I explored it. I made choices. I created something that didn't exist.”

The CCC method

Five steps, from dream to creation

  1. 01Imagine

    Find an original idea, ask questions, explore possibilities.

  2. 02Explore

    Discover what AI can do, how to talk to it, and where its limits begin.

  3. 03Create

    Turn the concept into a character, an image, a scene or a film.

  4. 04Question

    Spot the errors, biases and clichés, then decide for yourself.

  5. 05Share

    Present the creation and explain one's choices to others.

A two-hour session

A creative studio, not a computer class

Short 10- to 20-minute cycles: talk, draw, write, produce, present. Nobody stares at a screen for 35 minutes.

  1. 0 – 10 min
    The reveal

    A character goes from an idea to a finished image, in front of the group.

  2. 10 – 40 min
    Invent and design

    Personality, story, look: on paper first, in teams.

  3. 40 – 55 min
    Explore AI

    Describe the idea, compare results, critique, correct.

  4. 55 – 65 min
    Active break

    Move around, regroup.

  5. 65 – 85 min
    Tell a story

    A scene, a storyboard, a key moment for the character.

  6. 85 – 105 min
    Produce

    The final visuals and the group sequence.

  7. 105 – 115 min
    The premiere

    Each team presents its creation.

  8. 115 – 120 min
    Reflect

    What did the humans decide? What did the AI do?

Young people create the character and its visual identity during the workshop. The facilitator handles technical production and the final trailer edit, delivered after the session.

Responsible AI

Our rules, non-negotiable

You're trusting us with young people. Here's what we commit to, in every workshop.

  • No accounts for students

    The facilitator runs the AI tools. Students create no accounts.

  • No personal data

    No full names, addresses or school records go into an AI tool.

  • No real faces or voices

    We create fictional characters. No student photos or voices are used.

  • The teacher stays present

    We run the activity; responsibility for the class stays with the teacher.

  • Age-appropriate content

    Guided themes, no violent, sexualized or discriminatory content.

  • An offline plan B

    If a tool fails, the workshop continues with prepared materials.

Questions

What people ask us

Do students use AI themselves?

They write the descriptions, choose, critique and direct the revisions. The facilitator runs the generations from approved accounts: no student accounts, no personal data.

Does each student need a computer?

No. Much of the work happens on paper, in teams. We bring the production equipment; a shared screen is enough.

Who owns the creations?

The characters and stories belong to the young people. The group receives the gallery and the trailer for educational use. Our workbooks and method remain CCC Studios'.

Can a workshop be adapted to our theme?

Yes: environment, local history, a school mascot, the city's future… Custom themes and multi-session programs are prepared on quote.
